# NBS Template: Literary Prose

<capsule label="clash" trigger="physical violence happening now, blows landing (not: violence only threatened → coil; chase without fighting → pursuit)">
BEAT: Active physical contest. Violence is landing now.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Mid-close — inside the body, not the thoughts. Sensation and reflex are on the page; reflection is not.
VALUE SHIFT: Every exchange must change the tactical picture — ground gained or lost, a wound taken, a weapon dropped, an exit opened or closed. No exchange leaves the fight where it was.
ACTION ORDER: Stimulus before response, always: the blow lands, then the body reacts, then the counter. Never announce an action's result before the action.
SENTENCES: Short. 5–12 words average. One clause each. Fragments at impact.
PARAGRAPHS: 1–3 lines maximum. White space is pacing.
VERBS: Concrete and physical. Ban adverbs. "Slammed," not "moved quickly."
SENSORY: One detail per exchange — impact, breath, weapon feedback. Never stack two.
DIALOGUE: Clipped. Shouts, single words, broken sentences. No speeches while fighting.
TIME: Real-time or slower. Never summarize an exchange. Every hit lands on the page.
MICRO-EXAMPLE (register, not content): The pipe caught his shoulder. He went down, rolled, came up swinging — steel rang off brick where her head had been. Blood in his teeth. He spat and circled left.
FORBIDDEN: Adverbs, metaphor chains, "danced," "symphony of violence," wound inventories, narrator commentary on stakes, mid-action reflection.
END STATE: Close on a physical fact — a body position, a sound, a breath. Not a summary.
</capsule>

<capsule label="coil" trigger="held tension before a break — stealth, standoff, ambush not sprung, or stuck working an exit (not: a hit has landed → clash; fleeing under pressure → pursuit; passive horror with no action to take → dread)">
BEAT: Pre-break tension. Stealth, heist, standoff, ambush pending. Nothing has broken yet.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Close and narrow — locked inside dilated perception. Thought exists but only as tactics: distance, timing, exits.
VALUE SHIFT: The risk must tighten — each beat brings discovery one notch closer, or shrinks the window to act. If the danger level is flat across the response, the beat failed.
CORE RULE: The violence has NOT happened. The entire style is the held breath. Release belongs to clash.
FOCUS: Tight and procedural. Lock resistance, guard rotation, count of steps, sweat on the grip. Process detail creates tension.
TIME: Dilated hard. Seconds take paragraphs. A 30-second crossing can be the whole response.
SENSORY: Micro-scale. Heartbeat, fabric sound, a floorboard's give. The character hears everything.
NEAR-MISSES: The engine of the beat. One per response minimum — never a full break.
DIALOGUE: Whispers, hand signals, subvocal. Full-voice speech should feel like an alarm.
MICRO-EXAMPLE (register, not content): Third stair. The creak lived in the fourth. He waited out the guard's cough with his weight on the rail, then took the step — slow, slow — as the lantern light swung past the window and away.
FORBIDDEN: Resolving the tension, narrator reassurance, cutting away, "so far so good," time skips past the tense part.
END STATE: Close mid-tension or on the trigger-edge. Never on relief.
</capsule>

<capsule label="dread" trigger="horror or oppression on the page, mind still mostly maps reality (not: mundane inconvenience → coil/generic; hallucination-as-fact → fracture; soft dream blur → haze; grief without external threat → wound)">
BEAT: Looming threat, horror, oppression. Something is wrong and getting closer — not yet faced. Mind still mostly maps reality.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Mid, tightening closer as the wrongness accumulates. Report the world plainly; let the reader's alarm outrun the character's.
VALUE SHIFT: The wrongness must advance — one step nearer, or one layer harder to explain away. Each response removes one comfortable explanation.
PACING: Slow accumulation. Resist the payoff. The threat is stronger unseen.
ENVIRONMENT: Does the heavy lifting. Sound, smell, absence — birds that stopped, a door that was closed this morning. Wrongness in small details, never announced as wrong.
SENTENCES: Long and winding to build — then one short line that lands. Max once per 2–3 paragraphs.
THE SOURCE: Withhold it. Show effects, traces, aftermath. If it must appear, show a piece, never the whole.
INTERIORITY: Denial and rationalization. The character explains the wrongness away — the reader doesn't believe it.
LIGHT: No relief. No jokes, no comfort, no safe beats. If characters laugh, it's thin and dies fast.
MICRO-EXAMPLE (register, not content): The dogs had stopped barking sometime after midnight — all of them, all at once. She stood at the window with the cold coming off the glass and told herself that dogs sleep.
FORBIDDEN: "Suddenly," jump-scare punctuation, describing the threat fully, "an eerie feeling," narrator confirming danger before the character senses it, mundane inconvenience without horror cues.
END STATE: Close on a detail that is worse than the character admits.
</capsule>

<capsule label="passage" trigger="travel, exploring a place, noticing environment — discovery of space is the point (not: chase/escape → pursuit; overwhelm by vast/sacred scale → awe; transit that should be compressed → ledger)">
BEAT: Place and discovery as content. Travel, exploration, noticing environment.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Open and wide, zooming close only on found details. The camera belongs to the landscape more than the mind.
VALUE SHIFT: The map must grow — one new place, fact, or question that did not exist before this response. Movement without discovery is ledger's job, not this.
STRUCTURE: Vista → detail. Establish the wide shot, then zoom to one small found thing. Use this zoom once or twice per response.
MOMENTUM: Comes from novelty, not conflict. Each paragraph should show something new.
SENSES: Rotate them. Don't run three visual paragraphs in a row — give sound, air, footing.
TIME: Elastic. Compress the uneventful; expand discoveries.
CHARACTERS: React to the world rather than to each other. Dialogue is observational, sparse, unhurried.
INTERIORITY: Wonder, unease, memory triggered by place. Light, not dominant.
MICRO-EXAMPLE (register, not content): The valley opened below them, terraced in old stone that nobody had farmed in a century. Halfway down, a chimney was smoking.
FORBIDDEN: Conflict injection to "keep it interesting," encyclopedia lore-dumps, listing flora/fauna, describing the same vista twice, "breathtaking" as filler.
END STATE: Close on the small found detail — ideally one that asks a question.
</capsule>

<capsule label="pursuit" trigger="chase, escape, or racing a clock; movement plus urgency (not: quiet exploration → passage; fight already engaged → clash; hiding/holding still while hunted → coil)">
BEAT: Chase, escape, or race the clock. Movement plus urgency through space.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Close and kinetic — in the runner's breath and footing. Thought is navigation only.
VALUE SHIFT: The gap must change — closing, opening, or a route gained or lost. A chase paragraph that ends at the same distance it started is dead weight.
ENGINE: Every paragraph changes the gap, the path, or the clock.
SENTENCES: Forward-driving. Short bursts, with one longer line when terrain forces a choice.
VERBS: Motion verbs dominate — sprint, cut, vault, skid, double back. Ban floating description.
CAMERA: Tracks the runner and the pursuit pressure. Landmarks flash as navigation or obstacles only.
SENSORY: Breath, footing, noise behind. One obstacle detail at a time.
DIALOGUE: Shouts, clipped orders, breath-broken fragments. No speeches.
MICRO-EXAMPLE (register, not content): The fence was too high. Left — the loading dock. She vaulted a pallet, heard him hit the chain-link behind her swearing, and took the alley that smelled of rain and diesel. Two blocks to the river.
FORBIDDEN: Stopping to admire the view, summarizing the whole chase in one paragraph, letting the chase pause for talk without cost.
END STATE: Close on a new position or a narrowing option — door, alley, cliff edge, fuel gauge — not a full stop unless the catch lands.
</capsule>

<capsule label="flirt" trigger="flirtation, seduction, courtship, romantic tension without sex — attraction is the content but sex isn't happening now (not: clearly sexual now → heat; settled tenderness/comfort → ember; joke-first banter with no romantic charge → levity)">
BEAT: Flirtation, seduction, courtship, romantic tension without sex. The engine is the maybe — attraction unconfirmed or unconsummated.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Close — near enough to feel the flush and the held breath, but locked to one POV. Never inside both heads; the other person must stay legible only through surface signals, or the maybe dies.
VALUE SHIFT: The beat must move the maybe — bolder, safer, or interrupted. If the charge between them is the same at the end as at the start, the scene failed. Never static, never resolved.
SUBTEXT: Dominant. Dialogue is fencing: wit, dares, deflection, loaded innocence. Characters say less than they mean and mean more than they dare. Nobody states their attraction plainly — the reader decodes it.
PUSH-PULL: The structural rhythm is advance → retreat → counter. An offer half-withdrawn, a step closer then a turn away, a dare answered with a better dare. Each exchange shifts who is leaning in.
GESTURE: One charged micro-detail per exchange carries the current — fingers on a sleeve, a glass passed too slowly, eye contact held one beat too long. Never a catalog of features.
AWARENESS: POV hyper-attuned to the other body — heat, distance, breath, the space between hands — without explicit anatomy. Attraction is shown as quality of attention, not narration.
INTERIORITY: Denial and self-command, not confession. "She was not going to smile" outperforms "she felt drawn to him." Filtering verbs (felt, noticed, realized) weaken it — render the stimulus directly.
DIALOGUE: Fast, deflecting, daring. Questions answered with questions. The pause before a reply is content. Trailing off is content.
MICRO-EXAMPLE (register, not content): He held out the glass — then drew it back an inch. "Say please." She took it anyway, fingers dragging across his knuckles, and turned away before he could see her smile.
FORBIDDEN: Stating attraction outright, narrator confirming feelings are mutual, sexual escalation (that is heat's job), compliment stacking, blush-counting, resolving the tension into an established couple mid-beat.
END STATE: Close on the charged unresolved — a touch not taken, eye contact broken first, a door left open one inch.

<capsule label="politics" trigger="institutional power play — factions, policy, alliances, votes, boards, councils (not: pure personal spat → dispute; pure deal terms with no institutional board → negotiation; formal ceremony without faction play → court)">
BEAT: Institutional power play. Factions, policy, alliances, votes, boards, councils, collective strategy — any setting.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Mid to far — a room-reader's distance. The POV watches the board, not their own pulse. Cool, observant register.
VALUE SHIFT: The board must change — an alliance shifts, a cost surfaces, a vote moves, a debt is called. If the factional map is identical at the end, nothing happened.
CORE: The board is bigger than two people. Show who holds what leverage and who needs whom.
DIALOGUE: Strategic. Characters speak for blocs as much as for themselves. Offers come with costs.
INFORMATION: Partial and contested. Rumors, briefings, vote counts, loyalty signals. Narrator never settles the "true" map.
SOCIAL DETAIL: Track coalitions — who arrives together, who abstains, who changes sides mid-sentence.
INTERIORITY: Calculate, don't confess. POV may weigh outcomes; never lecture the reader on what it means.
PACING: Deliberate. Let a silence or a delayed answer move the faction board.
MICRO-EXAMPLE (register, not content): "The southern delegates will back the tariff," Voss said, refilling no one's glass but his own. "Provided the harbor contract reopens." So Ilsa had already reached them — which made her silence tonight a performance.
FORBIDDEN: Reducing politics to one villain monologue, explaining every motive aloud, collapsing into pure personal spat (dispute's job) or bare deal terms (negotiation's job).
END STATE: Close on a shifted alliance, a threatened vote, or an unresolved cost on the table.
</capsule>

<capsule label="court" trigger="formal protocol, ranked audience, or binding ritual sequence — hierarchy and prescribed steps ARE the pressure, not medieval-only (not: casual argument → dispute; haggling → negotiation; faction strategy meeting → politics; technical skilled work → machina)">
BEAT: Formal protocol, ranked audience, or binding ritual sequence. Throne hall, embassy, military review, corporate stage, temple rite, oath, summoning. Hierarchy and prescribed steps ARE the pressure. Not medieval-only.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Mid, surface-attentive — the camera reads faces, placements, and timing the way a courtier must. Interior readings stay uncertain.
VALUE SHIFT: Standing must change — someone rises, falls, is bound, or is exposed within the rules. A ceremony that ends with every rank where it started was scenery, not a scene.
CORE: Order and standing are visible architecture. Protocol and ritual procedure ARE the plot. If steps bind (vow, circle, offering), keep the sequence exact and show cost when broken.
DIALOGUE: Measured. Characters say less than they mean. Subtext mandatory in open audience. Formula and vows when the beat is ritual.
SOCIAL / RITUAL DETAIL: Who sits first, who may speak, who pours; or what is poured, cut, named, switched on. These details ARE the scene.
BODY LANGUAGE: Small and specific. A pause before answering. Fingers on a glass. No melodrama.
INTERIORITY: POV may read the room or the rite, never with certainty. "That seemed rehearsed" — not "He was lying."
PACING: Unhurried. Silence is a move. Each ritual step earns space.
MICRO-EXAMPLE (register, not content): The envoy bowed exactly low enough for a client state and not one degree lower. The Regent let the silence run before nodding — permission to stand, withheld one breath past courtesy.
FORBIDDEN: Narrator translating motives, villain monologues, "tension filled the room," casual argument (dispute's job), deal haggling without ceremony (negotiation's job).
END STATE: Close on an unresolved implication, a completed step, or an interrupted binding — something that changes standing.
</capsule>

<capsule label="negotiation" trigger="deal-making — bargain, contract, trade, hostage terms, quid pro quo; the object is terms (not: ceremony without a deal → court; interrogation without exchange → probe; faction board without a concrete deal → politics)">
BEAT: Deal-making. Bargain, contract, trade, hostage terms, quid pro quo. The object is terms.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Mid-close, ledger-visible — near enough to catch a tell, far enough to keep both offers in frame.
VALUE SHIFT: Terms must move — a concession lands, a demand dies, a deadline starts, a walk-away becomes real. If the table looks the same at the end, cut the beat.
CORE: Both sides want something exchangeable. Track offer → counter → concession → holdout.
DIALOGUE: Dominates. Numbers, conditions, deadlines, guarantees. What is on and off the table must stay clear to the reader.
STRUCTURE: Keep the ledger visible — what each side has offered so far; what still hurts.
INTERIORITY: Light. Prefer revealed preference through what they refuse, not essays on need.
NARRATOR: Do not declare who is winning. Show the last term that stuck.
BODY: Small tells only when they change a demand — a flinch at a price, a too-fast yes.
MICRO-EXAMPLE (register, not content): "Forty percent, and my people walk out armed." "Twenty. Sidearms only." "Thirty-five, and the ammunition stays with us." She looked at the crates a long moment. "The medic comes too, or there is no deal."
FORBIDDEN: Pure ceremony without terms (court's job), faction strategy without a deal object (politics' job), interrogation without exchange (probe's job).
END STATE: Close on a term accepted, rejected, or left hanging with a clock.
</capsule>

<capsule label="probe" trigger="extract or test under pressure — interrogation, cross-exam, cornering interview; the object is information or confession (not: fair personal argument → dispute; trading terms → negotiation; formal audience pageantry → court)">
BEAT: Extract or test under pressure. Interrogation, cross-exam, cornering interview. The object is information or confession.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Close on one side of the table — the presser's calculation or the pressured party's fear, not both.
VALUE SHIFT: The information state must change — a fact yielded, a lie caught, a refusal that itself reveals. Questions that produce nothing new are cut or made to cost something.
CORE: One side presses; the other resists, dodges, or breaks. Asymmetry of power or knowledge is the engine.
DIALOGUE: Question / evasion / follow-up. Every answer costs something or opens a new angle.
PRESSURE: Time, audience, evidence, threat of exposure — show the lever, don't only name it.
INTERIORITY: Narrow. Fear of the next question, calculation of what to give. No confession essays unless the break lands.
SENSORY: Room as pressure — light, chair, silence after a question. One concrete detail at a time.
MICRO-EXAMPLE (register, not content): "You said you left at nine." "I did." "The camera says nine forty." He didn't answer that. He looked at the mirror instead, at whoever stood behind it, and asked for water.
FORBIDDEN: Fair debate between equals with no extraction goal (dispute's job), deal trading (negotiation's job), protocol pageantry (court's job).
END STATE: Close on a crack — a new fact, a refused answer that reveals, or the next harder question.
</capsule>

<capsule label="dispute" trigger="personal verbal contest — argument, insult, private confrontation, not institutional/deal/interrogation (not: institutional/faction play → politics; formal protocol → court; deal terms → negotiation; interrogation for info → probe)">
BEAT: Personal verbal contest. Argument, insult, private confrontation. Not institutional, not a deal, not an interrogation.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Close and hot — inside one combatant's defensiveness. The other's blows land from outside.
VALUE SHIFT: The relationship temperature must change — a line crossed, a truth almost said, an old wound reopened, someone walks. An argument that ends where it began is filler.
CORE: Two (or few) people collide over hurt, blame, desire, or pride. The fight is interpersonal.
DIALOGUE: Fast and edged. Interruptions, callbacks to prior wounds, lines meant to land.
SUBTEXT: Present, but hotter and less ceremonial than court. People almost say the real thing.
BODY: Raised volume, turned backs, slammed objects — concrete, not melodramatic face-acting lists.
INTERIORITY: Heat and defensiveness. Short spikes, not therapy transcripts.
PACING: Escalate, then either cut or land one wounding line. Do not resolve into hugs unless the user action asks for it.

FORBIDDEN: Sliding into faction politics, formal protocol, term-sheet bargaining, or information extraction as the main goal — those belong to their own labels.
END STATE: Close on a line that changes the relationship temperature — said, unsaid, or walked away from.
</capsule>

INTERIORITY: Dominant. 50%+ of the prose lives in the character's head. The external scene is background.
DISTORTION: The world through the character's state — food has no taste, voices arrive from far away, time skips. Shown, never diagnosed.
UNDERSTATEMENT: The iron rule. Biggest hits get the smallest words. Melodrama is banned precisely because the beat invites it.
FRAGMENTS: Allowed sparingly. Max 2–3 per response.
BODY: Grief is physical. Heavy limbs, cold hands, inability to eat.
DIALOGUE: Minimal, flat, or absent.
MEMORY: Brief flashes — one image, not a flashback scene.

<capsule label="ledger" trigger="downtime, logistics, planning, shopping, travel prep — necessary connective tissue that should stay short and functional (not: dramatized skilled crisis procedure → machina; full bargain scene → negotiation; atmospheric travel discovery → passage)">
BEAT: Downtime, logistics, planning, shopping, travel prep. Necessary connective tissue.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Far — a quartermaster's report, not a lived scene. Brisk summary voice.
VALUE SHIFT: Readiness must change — a plan set, a resource gained or lost, a risk named. The party ends this beat better or worse prepared, and the reader knows which.
CORE RULE: Functional. Do not dramatize. Boring-on-purpose is correct.
COMPRESSION: Aggressive. Summarize freely. Skip transit, pleasantries, meals unless plot-relevant.
SENTENCES: Brisk, plain, informational. No atmosphere-building.
DETAIL BUDGET: Only what matters later. Every detail kept is a promise to the reader.
DIALOGUE: Compressed or summarized unless the exchange itself changes something — then write only that exchange.
PLANNING SCENES: Clear — who does what, what could go wrong, what they're missing.
LENGTH: Short. Half normal response length is fine.
MICRO-EXAMPLE (register, not content): Three days: powder from the quartermaster who didn't ask questions, horses from one who did, and a map that disagreed with the river in two places. They left before light on the fourth.
FORBIDDEN: Purple prose about mundane tasks, full haggling dialogues (negotiation's job if the bargain is the scene), dramatized skilled procedure (machina's job), padding to normal scene length.
END STATE: Close on the forward hook — plan set, departure, open risk.
</capsule>

<capsule label="levity" trigger="banter, comedy, deliberate respite; the pressure valve (not: tender quiet care → ember; romantic charge under the banter → flirt; serious argument → dispute)">
BEAT: Banter, comedy, respite. The pressure valve.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Mid, deadpan — the camera plays it straight while the situation is funny. Never wink.
VALUE SHIFT: The tension must vent — the room ends lighter, and the bond under the banter shows once. A comic beat that changes no one's temperature is dead air.
ENGINE: Timing. Setup → beat → punchline. White space before a landing line IS the joke.
DIALOGUE: Fast, dominant, minimal tags. "Said" or nothing. No adverb tags — "she quipped playfully" kills the joke it describes.
CHARACTERS: Allowed to be petty, dumb, stubborn, human. Comedy from flaws colliding — not narrator wit.
RHYTHM: Short exchanges. Interruptions, talking past each other, the pause before "...what."
PHYSICAL COMEDY: One concrete detail, precisely chosen. The dignity of the attempt, the totality of the failure.
SCENE DISCIPLINE: Get in, land it, get out. Shorter than a normal scene. End on the laugh or one beat after.
WARMTH: Underneath. The banter proves the bond.
MICRO-EXAMPLE (register, not content): "It's a simple plan." "You said that about the lighthouse." "The lighthouse was simple." "It's at the bottom of the harbor, Wick." "Simply sank."
FORBIDDEN: Narrator jokes, characters laughing at each other's lines, reference humor, explaining a joke, extending a bit past its landing, "hilarity," "antics."
END STATE: Close on the best line or the shared silence after it.
</capsule>

<capsule label="generic" trigger="ordinary continuation, mixed signals, or no specialty fits cleanly; the safe default when unsure, including mundane problems without horror or high drama">
BEAT: Default narration. Write a good story beat — no specialty mode required.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE: Flexible middle — close enough for character, wide enough for the room. Adjust naturally with the moment.
VALUE SHIFT: The scene must move one honest beat — a new fact, a reaction, a door opened — and leave the player something to answer. Never mark time.
GOAL: Clear, readable prose that advances the immediate moment. Trust concrete nouns and verbs.
SENTENCES: Natural mix of lengths. Prefer clarity over flourish.
DETAIL: Specific and grounded. Name what is in the room; skip filler atmosphere.
DIALOGUE: Characterful when present; do not pad with small talk.
INTERIORITY: Light and earned. Prefer action and reaction over essay.
PACING: One beat only. Enough for the player to respond; leave the scene open.
MICRO-EXAMPLE (register, not content): The innkeeper set down two bowls and didn't leave. "You'd be the ones asking about the north road," she said. "Whatever they told you in Varen, it's worse."
FORBIDDEN: Purple metaphor stacks, summarizing the whole arc, meta commentary, mechanical headers.
END STATE: Close on something the player can act on — a look, a question, a change in the room.
